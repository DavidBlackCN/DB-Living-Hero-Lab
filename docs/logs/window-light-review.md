## Stage 2 — projected daylight

### Algorithm and files

- `src/engine/lighting.ts`: `projectedLightAt()` interpolates window-side origin,
  ray direction, width, spread, reach and energy. Morning is narrow/steep and
  right-biased; noon starts higher and spreads broadly; dusk sweeps diagonally
  left onto the character and reading area. Energy is zero before 05:30 and
  after 19:30, with continuous transitions and midnight wrap.
- `src/engine/renderer.ts`: passes those keyframes as uniforms. Existing
  projected toggle/intensity/softness controls are retained; no new UI sliders.
- `src/engine/shaders.ts`: aspect-correct coordinates, two overlapping Gaussian
  aperture lobes, widening penumbra and finite reach. Receiver weights exclude
  glass and face; hair/cloth settings also affect their projected contribution.
  Turning off sun, projection or region refinement removes the projection.
  The result is artwork-directed 2D illumination, not a geometric shadow map.
- `docs/scene-regions.json` + generator + `scene-masks.svg`: green now represents
  indoor receivers, including desk (1), book (.90), cup (.75), stacked books
  (.65) and a conservative chair region (.40). Hands remain excluded. The
  existing green-weighted lamp/bloom eligibility now also follows those objects;
  this is a small intentional change to indoor response. The chair is available
  as a receiver but is largely beyond the current beam reach.
- `src/debug-ui/panel.ts`: corrected obsolete feature-status copy only.
- Neutral displays luminance × 0.6, fixed across all times, preventing the old
  default noon clipping. Compare Neutral **within** a stage; before/stage1 used
  scale 1. Final grayscale contact sheets use identical Rec.709 luma throughout.
- `tests/timeline.test.mjs` and `tests/visual/window-light.visual.spec.ts`: full-day
  continuity, zero night projection, controls, protected glass/face, object
  response and energy-normalized shape differences.
- `scripts/summarize-visual-review.py`: reproducible contact sheets, actual Final
  grayscale conversions, window before/after and capture metadata.

### Results

`stage2/acceptance` contains 08:00 / 12:00 / 17:30 / 23:00 × Final / Scene /
Overlay / Neutral / Projected, plus grayscale conversions and window closeups.
`projected-metrics.json` records GPU-readback measurements at 1200×675:

| Time | Projection centroid X/Y | Book coefficient | Cup coefficient |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 08:00 | 1052 / 525 | 0.000 | 0.231 |
| 12:00 | 942 / 515 | 0.126 | 0.208 |
| 17:30 | 821 / 458 | 0.438 | 0.376 |
| 23:00 | no projection | 0 | 0 |

Dusk shoulder and hair samples are 0.346 / 0.476; face and glass are zero.
After normalizing each projection to equal mean energy, mean absolute shape
differences are 0.967 (morning/noon) and 0.880 (noon/dusk), exceeding the 0.3
regression threshold. These prove position/shape changes, not artistic quality;
the latter still needs human inspection of Final and its grayscale version.

`npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm test` (7/7) and
`npm run test:visual` (25/25) passed. The full suite includes DPR and bloom checks.
No WebGL/page errors in checked cases. Existing source texture estimate remains
94.92 MiB; the embedded flower coverage is baked into the existing scene texture,
not a new GPU sampler/pass. Headless DPR 1.5 still measured about 50 ms median
RAF intervals: this is a pre-existing performance limitation, not a 60 FPS claim.

Both approved source SHA-256 values match the original phase1 record. No change
to the normal map or character mask, no character animation or Blog integration.

### Conclusion

The reported right lower frame/sill leak is corrected at its source. Final,
Scene and Overlay agree on the same coverage. Fine flowers/transparent objects
remain approximate. Noon/dusk now have visibly different receiver structure;
the low-frequency normal limits convincing hair strands, fabric folds and cup
volume. Baked source lighting is a separate limitation that a better normal alone
cannot remove. After manual acceptance, prioritize a registered high-quality
normal; then recalibrate the projection modestly. Do not migrate to Blog yet.
